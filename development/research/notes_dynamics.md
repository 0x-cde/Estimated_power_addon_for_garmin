# Cycling dynamics — verified sources (working notes)

1. Martin JC, Milliken DL, Cobb JE, McFadden KL, Coggan AR (1998). Validation of a mathematical model for road cycling power. J Appl Biomech 14(3):276-291. doi:10.1123/jab.14.3.276. Free PDF Univ. Utah. READ:
   - P = [½ρ(CdA+Fw)Va²Vg + Vg Crr m g cos + Vg(91+8.7Vg)e-3 + Vg m g sin + ½(m+I/r²)ΔV²/Δt] / Ec
   - Fw = 0.0044 m² (worked example: CdA 0.2565 + 0.0044); bearings from Dahn et al 1991 torque 0.015+0.00005·rpm N·m per pair; I(two wheels) ≈ 0.14 kg·m² (≈1.4-1.5 kg effective mass at r≈0.31 m); Ec = 0.976981 (SRM vs Monark ergometer regression slope, i.e. 2.36% losses incl. chain); Crr 0.0032 (mean of Kyle 1988, 10 clinchers 0.0027-0.0040 on smooth asphalt).
   - Wind tunnel CdA (TT bike) 0.269/0.265/0.265/0.255 at 0/5/10/15° yaw (n.s.).
   - Model vs SRM R² 0.97, SE 2.7 W, mean 172.0 vs 172.8 W; rolling 10-20% of power on flat; KE ~1%, bearings ~1%.
   - Wind measured 800 m away; within-bout SD 0.4 m/s — wind is the main error source.
2. di Prampero PE, Cortili G, Mognoni P, Saibene F (1979). Equation of motion of a cyclist. J Appl Physiol 47(1):201-206. doi:10.1152/jappl.1979.47.1.201. Towing tests: RT = 3.2 + 0.19 v² (N); W = 4.5e-2·P·s + 4.1e-2·SA·(PB/T)·v²·s.
3. Spicer JB, Richardson CJK, Ehrlich MJ, Bernstein JR, Fukuda M, Terada M (2001). Effects of frictional loss on bicycle chain drive efficiency. J Mech Des 123(4):598-605. doi:10.1115/1.1412848. Efficiency (numeric range not read — paywalled) depends on chain tension & sprocket size, lubrication ~negligible in lab; higher tension -> higher efficiency.
4. Danek T, Slawinski MA, Stanoev T (2020). On modelling bicycle power-meter measurements: Part I. Estimating effects of air, rolling and drivetrain resistance. arXiv:2005.04229. Part II arXiv:2005.04480. Fit CdA, Crr, drivetrain from power-meter + GPS.
5. Slawinski et al (2020). On modelling bicycle power for velodromes Part I. arXiv:2005.04691 (context).
6. Kyle CR, Berto F (2001). The mechanical efficiency of bicycle derailleur and hub-gear transmissions. Human Power 52:3-11. Free PDF ihpva.org. Derailleurs 87-97%, hubs 86-95% at 80-200 W; efficiency rises with power; cross-chaining minor. Rebuttal Rohloff, Human Power 55:11-15 (95-98.5% at 400 W; ergometer losses).
7. Maier T, Schmid L, Müller B, Steiner T, Wehrlin JP (2017). Accuracy of cycling power meters against a mathematical model of treadmill cycling. Int J Sports Med 38(6):456-461. doi:10.1055/s-0043-102945. 54 power meters / 9 brands; deviations up to 16.5% (as cited) -> "ground truth" power meters themselves err by a few %.
8. Bouillod A, Soto-Romero G, Grappe F, Bertucci W, Brunet E, Cassirame J (2022). Caveats and recommendations to assess the validity and reliability of cycling power meters: a systematic scoping review. Sensors 22(1):386. OA. 74 studies.
9. Dahmen T, Byshko R, Saupe D, Röder M, Mantler S (2011). Validation of a model and a simulator for road cycling on real tracks. Sports Eng 14:95-110. doi:10.1007/s12283-011-0076-1. Martin model on mountain tracks r 0.96-0.99. Free PDF Uni Konstanz.
10. Dahmen T, Saupe D (2011). Calibration of a power-speed-model for road cycling using real power and height data. (icSPORTS?) — individual parameter set from calibration rides.
11. Sitko S, López-Laval I, Cirer-Sastre R, Barrajón-Pérez C, Legaz-Arrese A (2026). From speed to watts: a physics-based model for estimating power output during professional cycling climbs. J Sports Med Phys Fitness 66(9):1032-1038. doi:10.23736/S0022-4707.26.18115-8. 28 pros, MAE 14.1 W, MAPE 3.65%, bias -5.4 W on uphill TT. Shows climbs are the easy case.
12. Olds TS, Norton KI, Craig NP (1993). Mathematical model of cycling performance. J Appl Physiol 75(2):730-737. doi:10.1152/jappl.1993.75.2.730.
13. Olds TS, Norton KI, Lowe EL, Olive S, Reay F, Ly S (1995). Modeling road-cycling performance. J Appl Physiol 78(4):1596-1611. doi:10.1152/jappl.1995.78.4.1596. Includes wind, tyre pressure, wheel radius, altitude, humidity, rotational KE, drafting.
14. Jeukendrup AE, Martin JC (2001). Improving cycling performance: how should we spend our time and money. Sports Med 31(7):559-569. Model of 40 km TT time savings; CdA upright 0.358 / dropped 0.307 / TT 0.240-0.269 (as compiled by Defraeye).
15. Chung R (2012). Estimating CdA with a power meter (virtual elevation). Presentation, not peer reviewed.
16. Dyer B, Disley BX? (2018). Validation of the virtual elevation field test method when assessing the aerodynamics of para-cyclists with a uni-lateral trans-tibial amputation. Disabil Rehabil Assist Technol 13(2). doi:10.1080/17483107.2017.1297857. VE CV 2-3% vs tunnel 0.7-0.9%; 0.03 m2 absolute offset. (author list to confirm)
17. Menaspà P, Impellizzeri FM, Haakonssen EC, Martin DT, Abbiss CR (2014). Consistency of commercial devices for measuring elevation gain. IJSPP 9(5):884-886. doi:10.1123/ijspp.2013-0232. Garmin vs SRM differ up to 3%; Garmin elevation correction +5-10%.
18. Menaspà P, Haakonssen E, Sharma A, Clark B (2016). Accuracy in measurement of elevation gain in road cycling. JSC 5(1):10-12. Garmin underestimates ~2% dry, bias to -25% in wet weather (barometer port water).
19. Atkinson G, Peacock O, Passfield L (2007). Variable versus constant power strategies during cycling time-trials: prediction of time savings using an up-to-date mathematical model. J Sports Sci 25(9):1001-1009. doi:10.1080/02640410600944709. Martin-model based; ±10% power with gradient/wind saves 26 s / 40 km.
20. Cohen C, Brunet E, Roy J, Clanet C (2021). Physics of road cycling and the three jerseys problem. J Fluid Mech 914:A38. doi:10.1017/jfm.2020.1022. OA (HAL). Flat (aero), climb (gravity) regimes; phase diagram.
21. Groeskamp S (2017). Translating uphill cycling into a head-wind and vice versa. JSC 6(1):32-37. Incline-equivalent wind velocity.
22. Dahn K, Mai L, Poland J, Jenkins C (1991). Frictional resistance in bicycle wheel bearings. Cycling Science 3(3):28-32. Basis of Martin's (91+8.7V)e-3 W bearing term; cartridge+grease+seal much higher than cup-and-cone oil.
23. Kyle CR (1986). Mechanical factors affecting the speed of a cycle. In Burke ER (ed) Science of Cycling, Human Kinetics, pp 123-136. / Kyle CR (1988). The mechanics and aerodynamics of cycling. In Burke & Newsom (eds) Medical and Scientific Aspects of Cycling, pp 235-252 (source of Crr 0.0027-0.0040 for 10 clinchers).
24. Wilson DG, Schmidt T, Papadopoulos J (2020). Bicycling Science, 4th ed. MIT Press. Standard reference.
25. Faria et al (2005) Sports Med 35(4):313-337 (see rolling 26). Lim et al (2011) (rolling 25). Candau et al (1999) (rolling 2). Grappe et al (1997) (cda 23).
26. PLOS ONE (2023): By cyclists, for cyclists: road grade and elevation estimation from crowd-sourced fitness application data. doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0295027 (authors to verify).
Grey: Strava estimates (support page: weight, speed, elevation only); informal comparisons (road.cc 2025: 153 vs 180 W; Bales 2014; VeloViewer) — not peer reviewed; consistent pattern: good on long climbs, low on flats/rough/off-road.
