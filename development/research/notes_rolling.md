# Rolling resistance — verified sources (working notes)

1. Grappe F, Candau R, Barbier B, Hoffman MD, Belli A, Rouillon JD (1999). Influence of tyre pressure and vertical load on coefficient of rolling resistance and simulated cycling performance. Ergonomics 42(10):1361-1371. doi:10.1080/001401399185009. Indoor deceleration. Cr = 0.1071*P^-0.477 (r2 0.99; pressure unit not confirmed — paywalled). Cr varies ~20% over 600-900 kPa; +15 kg raised Cr (2nd-order polynomial). Access: RG PDF.
2. Candau RB et al. (1999). Simplified deceleration method for assessment of resistive forces in cycling. MSSE 31(10):1441-1447. Coast-down in hallways; reproducibility 0.66% CdA / 0.70% Crr; Crr 0.00368 (road tyre, indoor floor); CdA aero head up 0.304, in line 0.268, intermediate 0.262 m2; Crr rises with external load.
3. Rothhämel M (2023). On rolling resistance of bicycle tyres with ambient temperature in focus. IJVSMT 17(1):67-80. doi:10.1504/IJVSMT.2023.132317. Pendulum rig, -30..+25 C. Pressure and temperature each change Crr by factor 2-3 in relevant range; vertical load minor; wider tyre ~10% lower Crr (same model). Free PDF inderscience.com/storage/f102741185312916.pdf
4. Hyttinen J, Rothhämel M, Jerrelind J, Drugge L (2024). Simulation of transient rolling resistance of bicycle tyres at various ambient temperatures. PLoS ONE 19(6):e0302821. Open access. Tyre warms during ride; energy demand much higher at -30 C than +20 C.
5. Fenre MD, Klein-Paste A (2021). Rolling resistance measurements on cycleways using an instrumented bicycle. J Cold Reg Eng 35(2):04021001. doi:10.1061/(ASCE)CR.1943-5495.0000244. OA. Power meter + Aeropod (pitot) energy balance; Crr precision ±0.001-0.005; accuracy 96.5%.
6. Fenre MD, Klein-Paste A (2021). Bicycle rolling resistance under winter conditions. Cold Reg Sci Technol 187:103282. Median Crr 0.010-0.056 in winter conditions (studded 42 mm, 2 bar).
7. Henchoz Y, Crivelli G, Borrani F, Millet G (2010). A new method to measure rolling resistance in treadmill cycling. J Sports Sci 28(10):1043-1046. doi:10.1080/02640414.2010.498483. Slope-equilibrium method; 28 mm ~0.002 higher Crr than 22 mm on treadmill (per citing paper - verify).
8. Reiser RF, Watt JM, Peterson ML (2003). Cycling on rollers: influence of tyre pressure and cross section on power requirements. Sports Biomech 2(2):237-249. doi:10.1080/14763140308522821. Rollers: power rises with each pressure drop; 20c worse than 23-28c; width differences NOT observed on road.
9. Baldissera P, Delprete C (2017). Rolling resistance, vertical load and optimal number of wheels in human-powered vehicle design. Proc IMechE P 231(1):33-42. doi:10.1177/1754337115625002. Crr strongly load dependent for cycling wheels (literature + experiments).
10. Baldissera P, Delprete C, Rossi M, Zahar A (2021). Experimental comparison of speed-dependent rolling coefficients in small cycling tires. Tire Sci Technol 49(3):224-241. doi:10.2346/tire.20.190207. Crr nonlinear in speed 45-145 km/h; latex vs butyl up to -54% losses; latex ≈ tubeless.
11. Dell'Orto G, Ballo F, Mastinu G, Gobbi M, Magnani G (2023). Racing bicycle tyres - influence on mechanical characteristics of internal pressure, vertical force, speed and temperature. Eur J Mech A/Solids (S099775382300102X). VeTyT rig.
12. Dell'Orto G, Mastinu G (2022). Effect of temperature on the mechanical characteristics of bicycle tyres. Conference paper, TU Delft repository. Higher T -> lower Crr (citing earlier work).
13. Ejsmont JA, Ronowski G, Świeczko-Żurek B, Sommer S (2017). Road texture influence on tyre rolling resistance. Road Mater Pavement Des 18(1):181-198. doi:10.1080/14680629.2016.1160835. MPD 0.20-4.75 mm; Crr correlates with MPD but not strongly / nonlinear (tread envelopment). (car tyres)
14. Ejsmont J et al. (2021). Selected aspects of pavement texture influence on tire rolling resistance. Coatings 11(7):776. doi:10.3390/coatings11070776. OA.
15. Ejsmont J et al. (2024). Comparison of tire rolling resistance measuring methods for different surfaces. Int J Automot Technol. doi:10.1007/s12239-024-00092-w. Steel-drum ISO/SAE results correlate poorly with road (values AND rankings); road-replica drum surfaces improve it.
16. Sandberg U (ed.), Bergiers A, Ejsmont J et al. (2011). Road surface influence on tyre/road rolling resistance. MIRIAM SP1-04 report, VTI.
17. Bertucci WM, Rogier S, Reiser RF (2013). Evaluation of aerodynamic and rolling resistances in mountain-bike field conditions. J Sports Sci 31(14):1606-1613. doi:10.1080/02640414.2013.792945. Smooth vs knobby: smooth 21±15% lower Cr; road/sand/grass; 200/400 kPa; MTB CdA 0.357±0.023 m2; aero 8-35% of resistance.
18. Steyn WJvdM, Warnich J (2014). Comparison of tyre rolling resistance for different mountain bike tyre diameters and surface conditions. S Afr J Res Sport Phys Educ Recreat 36(2):179-193. OA (UP repository). Coast-down; bitumen/gravel/grass/sand; surface largest effect, then mass, wheel diameter, pressure; 29" lower than 26".
19. Macdermid PW, Fink PW, Stannard SR (2015). The influence of tyre characteristics on measures of rolling performance during cross-country mountain biking. J Sports Sci 33(3):277-285. doi:10.1080/02640414.2014.942684 (DOI conflict with ...682 - check).
20. Macdermid PW, Fink PW, Stannard SR (2015). Tyre volume and pressure effects on impact attenuation during mountain bike riding. Shock and Vibration 2015:191075. OA.
21. Buder J, Fouchard E, Schwanitz S (2025). The impact of tyre width, pressure and surface condition on rolling resistance and vibration transmission of bicycle tyres. JSC 14(2) art 12 (conference abstract). Treadmill, 23-32 mm, tarmac/cobbles, 4-8.5 bar; nonlinear width x pressure; wider tyres reduce vibration.
22. Sellier M, Bellenoue S (2025). A novel energy imbalance approach applied to rolling resistance assessment in cycling. JSC 14(2):17 (conference abstract). Field, 100 m sections, <2% repeatability; +26% Crr 6->3 bar; TT vs non-TT tyre 33%; 9% between manufacturers; +26% cold (14 C) vs warm (18 C) tyre.
23. Tengattini S, Bigazzi AY (2018). Validation of an outdoor coast-down test to measure bicycle resistance parameters. J Transp Eng A 144(7):04018031. doi:10.1061/JTEPBS.0000152. Free PDF UBC REACT lab. 12 time traps over 100 m; models varying wind and grade; outdoor Crr 0.0064±0.0013, CdA 0.63±0.11 (upright commuter).
24. Tengattini S, Bigazzi AY (2018). Physical characteristics and resistance parameters of typical urban cyclists. J Sports Sci 36(20):2383-2391. doi:10.1080/02640414.2018.1458587. 557 Vancouver cyclists: Crr 0.0077 (SD 0.0036), CdA 0.559 (SD 0.170) m2, bike+cargo 18.3 kg, bike 13.7 kg. Free PDF UBC.
25. Lim AC, Homestead EP, Edwards AG, Carver TC, Kram R, Byrnes WC (2011). Measuring changes in aerodynamic/rolling resistances by cycle-mounted power meters. MSSE 43(5):853-860. Power meters detect changes in position (CdA) and tyre pressure (Crr) in the field.
26. Faria EW, Parker DL, Faria IE (2005). The science of cycling: factors affecting performance - Part 2. Sports Med 35(4):313-337. doi:10.2165/00007256-200535040-00003. Review incl. rolling resistance, aero.
27. Buder J, Schwanitz S, Odenwald S (2024). Benchmarking rolling resistance of bicycle tyres on a laboratory treadmill. Engineering of Sport 15 (ISEA 2024), Loughborough repository 27045271. 15 WTB tyres, treadmill instead of drum.
28. Crenna F et al. (2025). A measurement system to characterize the effects of tires on bicycle vibrations. Eng 6(9):245. doi:10.3390/eng6090245. OA. 24/28/32 mm, 4/6 bar, tube vs tubeless, 25/40 km/h: speed > pressure > width > tube on vibration. (Vibration only, no Crr.)
29. Turner M (2024). Cycling on rough roads: a model for resistance and vibration. arXiv:2405.00019. "Roughness resistance" = damping in rider body; links IRI to resistance; lower vertical stiffness reduces it.
Industry / grey (methods, not data):

- Heine J / Bicycle Quarterly / Rene Herse blog: suspension losses mostly in rider; claims up to 290 W on very rough road (informal).
- Poertner J / SILCA: impedance = drum hysteresis + accelerometer-derived losses (whitepaper, informal).
