# Rider and bike CdA — verified sources (working notes)

1. Bassett DR Jr, Kyle CR, Passfield L, Broker JP, Burke ER (1999). Comparing cycling world hour records, 1967-1996: modeling with empirical data. MSSE 31(11):1665-1676. doi:10.1097/00005768-199911000-00025. Total frontal area (rider+bike, aero-bar position) from DuBois BSA; equation images not extractable from the copy read, but Af = 0.0293 H^0.725 M^0.425 + 0.0604 reproduces the paper's stated example (H 1.82 m, M 75.4 kg -> Af 0.344 m2) exactly - VERIFIED indirectly. Crr track ~0.0035 (0.0022 smooth minimum); A2 (bearings/windage) 0.00775 W/(km/h)^2... from Moore 17" wheel. Polytropic density formula n=1.235. Factors: Obree 0.96, Superman 0.95 vs standard aero, drop bars + spoked wheels (Merckx bike) 1.18, aero frame 0.93. Model error avg 3.9% (5 tracks).
2. Heil DP (2001). Body mass scaling of projected frontal area in competitive cyclists. Eur J Appl Physiol 85(3-4):358-366. doi:10.1007/s004210100424. 21 riders, aero bars. Ap = 0.00433 STA^0.172 TA^0.0965 m^0.762 (SEE 0.017 m2). CdA ∝ m^0.312.
3. Heil DP (2002). Body mass scaling of frontal area in competitive cyclists not using aero-handlebars. Eur J Appl Physiol 87:520-528.
4. Debraux P, Grappe F, Manolova AV, Bertucci W (2011). Aerodynamic drag in cycling: methods of assessment. Sports Biomech 10(3):197-218. doi:10.1080/14763141.2011.592209. Review: wind tunnel, coast-down, linear regression, power-meter field methods, frontal area by photo/weighing/digitising. Cd varies between individuals, not proportional to Ap.
5. Debraux P, Bertucci W, Manolova AV, Rogier S, Lodini A (2009). New method to estimate the cycling frontal area. Int J Sports Med 30:266-272.
6. Crouch TN, Burton D, LaBry ZA, Blair KB (2017). Riding against the wind: a review of competition cycling aerodynamics. Sports Eng 20(2):81-110. doi:10.1007/s12283-017-0234-1. OA (MIT DSpace).
7. García-López J, Rodríguez-Marroyo JA, Juneau CE, Peleteiro J, Córdova A, Villa JG (2008). Reference values and improvement of aerodynamic drag in professional cyclists. J Sports Sci 26(3):277-286. doi:10.1080/02640410701501697. Wind tunnel, 5 pros: position changes -14%; aero helmet not favourable for all; CV<2%.
8. Defraeye T, Blocken B, Koninckx E, Hespel P, Carmeliet J (2010). Aerodynamic study of different cyclist positions: CFD analysis and full-scale wind-tunnel tests. J Biomech 43(7):1262-1268. doi:10.1016/j.jbiomech.2010.01.025. Upright/dropped/TT. Preprint urbanphysics.net.
9. Malizia F, Blocken B (2020). Bicycle aerodynamics: history, state-of-the-art and future perspectives. J Wind Eng Ind Aerodyn 200:104134. doi:10.1016/j.jweia.2020.104134. OA. Frame, wheels, handlebar, equipment.
10. Garimella R, Peeters T, Parrilla E, Uriel J, Sels S, Huysmans T, Verwulgen S (2020). Estimating cycling aerodynamic performance using anthropometric measures. Appl Sci 10(23):8635. doi:10.3390/app10238635. OA. Frontal area from 26 anthropometrics + joint angles, RMSE 0.032-0.037 m2.
11. Candau RB et al. (1999) MSSE 31(10) - see rolling notes: CdA aero head up 0.304 / in line 0.268 / intermediate 0.262 m2.
12. Bertucci WM, Rogier S, Reiser RF (2013) - MTB CdA 0.357±0.023 m2 (see rolling notes).
13. Tengattini & Bigazzi (2018) - urban cyclists CdA 0.559 (SD 0.170) m2; validation test 0.63±0.11 (see rolling notes).
14. Lim et al. (2011) - field power-meter detection of position change (see rolling notes).
15. Padilla S, Mujika I, Angulo F, Goiriena JJ (2000). Scientific approach to the 1-h cycling world record: a case study. J Appl Physiol 89(4):1522-1527. doi:10.1152/jappl.2000.89.4.1522. Frontal area taken as a fixed fraction (~18%, per search snippet, not read) of DuBois BSA; argues Bassett underestimates bigger riders' power.
16. Martin JC, Gardner AS, Barras M, Martin DT (2006). Modeling sprint cycling using field-derived parameters and forward integration. MSSE 38(3):592-597. doi:10.1249/01.mss.0000193560.34022.04. + companion: Martin et al (2006) Aerodynamic drag area of cyclists determined with field-based measures. Sportscience 10:68-69.
17. Barry N, Burton D, Sheridan J, Thompson MC, Brown NAT (2015). Aerodynamic performance and riding posture in road cycling and triathlon. Proc IMechE P 229(1):28-38. doi:10.1177/1754337114549876. 9 postures; hoods with horizontal forearms cuts required power 13.4% (vs upright?); short aerobars up to -16.7%; head/eyes down increased drag in drops and TT.
18. Blocken B, Defraeye T, Koninckx E, Carmeliet J, Hespel P (2013). CFD simulations of the aerodynamic drag of two drafting cyclists. Comput Fluids 71:435-445. doi:10.1016/j.compfluid.2012.11.012. UP/DP/TTP; leading rider also gains (~1.3-1.6% at 0.15 m).
19. Blocken B et al. (2018). Aerodynamic drag in cycling pelotons: new insights by CFD simulation and wind tunnel testing. J Wind Eng Ind Aerodyn 179:319-337 (ORBi 2268/226177; verify pages). (drafting - not modelled, context only)
20. Fintelman DM, Sterling M, Hemida H, Li FX (2014). The effect of crosswinds on cyclists: an experimental study. Procedia Eng 72:720-725. doi:10.1016/j.proeng.2014.06.122. 0-90° yaw; force coefficients strongly yaw dependent.
21. Fintelman DM, Hemida H, Sterling M, Li FX (2015). CFD simulations of the flow around a cyclist subjected to crosswinds. J Wind Eng Ind Aerodyn. doi:10.1016/j.jweia.2015.05.009. Side force up to ~2.5x drag (spoked wheels, dropped); bike ~60% of side force at large yaw.
22. Greenwell DI, Wood NJ, Bridge EKL, Addy RJ (1995). Aerodynamic characteristics of low-drag bicycle wheels. Aeronaut J 99(983):109-120. doi:10.1017/S0001924000028281. 7 wheels; wheels ~10-15% of total drag; disc saves ~0.013 m2 (Tew & Sayers 1999: 0.040).
23. Grappe F, Candau R, Belli A, Rouillon JD (1997). Aerodynamic drag in field cycling with special reference to the Obree's position. Ergonomics 40(12):1299-1311. doi:10.1080/001401397187388. vs upright: dropped -7.8%, TT -12.4%, Obree -27.8% (as reported by Lukes 2005 review - secondary).
24. Capelli C, Rosa G, Butti F, Ferretti G, Veicsteinas A, di Prampero PE (1993). Energy cost and efficiency of riding aerodynamic bicycles. Eur J Appl Physiol 67(2):144-149. doi:10.1007/BF00376658. Drag constant 0.155-0.157 (aero) vs 0.184 N s2/m2 (traditional) ~15% lower; lenticular wheels negligible.
25. Peterman JE, Lim AC, Ignatz RI, Edwards AG, Byrnes WC (2015). Field-measured drag area is a key correlate of level cycling time trial performance. PeerJ 3:e1144. doi:10.7717/peerj.1144. OA. Projected frontal area explained only 54% of CdA variability.
26. Barelle C, Chabroux V, Favier D (2010). Modeling of the time trial cyclist projected frontal area incorporating anthropometric, postural and helmet characteristics. Sports Eng 12(4):199-206. doi:10.1007/s12283-010-0047-y. Height R2 0.81, mass 0.75 predictors of A; helmet ~4%.
27. Lukes RA, Chin SB, Haake SJ (2005). The understanding and development of cycling aerodynamics. Sports Eng 8(2):59-74. doi:10.1007/BF02844004. Review; aero ~90% of resistance at ~50 km/h; MTB aero share 50% at 8.75 m/s (as cited).
28. Brownlie L et al. (2009). Streamlining the time trial apparel of cyclists: the Nike Swift Spin project. Sports Eng. Skinsuits: 7% best vs worst (Brownlie 1991/1992); textured fabrics.
29. Oggiano L, Brownlie L, Troynikov O, Bardal LM, Sæter C, Sætran L (2013). A review on skin suits and sport garment aerodynamics: guidelines and state of the art. Procedia Eng 60:91-98 (verify pages).
30. Hopker JG, Barnes C, Fennell CRJ, Gibson S (2025). Validity and reliability of an on-bike sensor system for the determination of aerodynamic drag in cycling. Eur J Sport Sci 25(12):e70098. doi:10.1002/ejsc.70098. OA. Body Rocket: smallest change 0.002 m2, CV 1.67%.
31. Malizia F, Blocken B (2021). Cyclist aerodynamics through time: better, faster, stronger. J Wind Eng Ind Aerodyn 214:104673.

## Numbers extracted from Defraeye et al. 2010 preprint (read full text)
Wind tunnel 10 m/s, one cyclist, static (no pedalling), disc wheels, Cervélo Soloist:
- CdA rider+bike: UP 0.270, DP 0.243, TTP 0.211 m2; rider-only 0.193/0.167/0.134; rider share 72/68/64 %.
- Frontal areas UP 0.41, DP 0.37, TTP 0.34 m2  => Cd(total) ≈ 0.66 / 0.66 / 0.62.
- CFD RANS 7-13% high vs tunnel; LES 3-13%.

- Kyle & Burke 1984: dropped 0.32, crouched 0.25, hill descent 0.23 (static, rho 1.206)
- Zdravkovich 1996 (rider only): upright 0.26-0.38, dropped 0.23-0.34, TT 0.17-0.23
- Martin et al 1998: TT 0.269 ± 0.006 (dynamic)
- Jeukendrup & Martin 2001: upright 0.358, dropped 0.307, TT 0.240-0.269
- García-López 2008: TT 0.260 ± 0.024 static vs 0.341 ± 0.030 dynamic (pedalling) — pedalling adds a lot
- Gibertini 2008: dropped 0.275, TT 0.223 (dynamic)
- Broker & Kyle 1995: TT 0.203; Kyle 1991: TT 0.221; Padilla 2000: TT 0.244
- TT average across studies 0.23 ± 0.03
